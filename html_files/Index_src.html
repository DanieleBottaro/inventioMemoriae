Inventio Memoriae

Mapping the construction of Memory: A Linked Open Data approach to Nicola Pisano’s Arca di San Domenico

Created by: Daniele Bottaro
Course in Information Science & Cultural Heritage

Domain Analysis

The project aims to model and represent in Linked Open Data (LOD) format the iconography chosen by the Dominicans to decorate the panels of the sarcophagus of the Arca di San Domenico by Nicola Pisano, as a visual consecration and monumental objectification of a process of "memory construction."

The data architecture traces the trajectory of this complex process brilliantly brought to light by Luigi Canetti in L'invenzione della memoria. Il culto e l'immagine di Domenico nella storia dei primi frati predicatori: the transition leading from the actual historical event to its intentional reworking in hagiographic sources (particularly in the narration of miracles), ultimately culminating in its monumentalization between 1264 and 1267 in the sarcophagus of the Arca.

To populate the network of relationships that makes up the project, the entities were extracted directly from two texts. The aforementioned essay by Canetti provided the ideal references to identify the web of historical figures, hagiographic texts, and events, as well as their correlations. However, since the author only touches upon the creation of the monument in passing, it was necessary to supplement this with an art-historical source in order to map its physical objectification. Therefore, Anita F. Moskovitz's authoritative monograph on Nicola Pisano's work (Nicola Pisano's arca di San Domenico and its legacy) was chosen, which provided the textual basis necessary to extract and describe the entities closely linked to the sarcophagus and the individual narrative panels.

Finally, to tie together this complex network of relationships and provide a broader historical and conceptual context, it was decided to integrate into the model several ad hoc entities created in an external JSON file, also with the aim of preserving the structural and semantic integrity of the ontologies.

Entities extraction and modeling

A total of 93 entities were identified and formally modeled for this project. Among these, 46 were directly extracted from selected snippets of the two textual sources, while the remaining 47 contextual entities were defined within the supplementary JSON dataset. To facilitate exploration, the entities have been classified into seven macro-categories: People, Events, Artworks, Narrative Themes, Literary Works, Visual Items, and Others.

Each entity is presented through an interactive flip card. The front of the card displays the primary metadata: the entity's title (rdfs:label), its project-specific local identifier, and any associated authority control links (such as VIAF, Wikidata, or GeoNames for spatial entities). The back of the card provides a comprehensive, bilingual description (dcterms:description) in both English and Italian.

The entity gallery below was generated dynamically using the Jinja2 templating engine. This data-driven approach ensures complete scalability: if new entities are added to either the TEI-XML or the JSON file in the future, they will automatically populate this web interface simply by re-running the jinja_sorting.py script (you can find it here).

Knowledge Organization

Theoretical Model

A theoretical model was created in order to define the relationships between the entities. Here are some of the main categories that entities fall into:

  • Artworks: the Sarcophagus of the Arca di san Domenico (the object we are investigating as the material culmination of the process of the “invention of memory”) and its breakdown into panels;
  • Literary works: the majority are hagiographic works, included because they are the source or vehicle for the transmission of the miraculous events depicted on the sarcophagus panels;
  • People: they can be the authors of literary works, or the subjects/participants in the mapped miraculous events, or figures depicted on the narrative panels;
  • Visual items: the panels depict visual elements (visual compositions) — often composed of other visual items (visual details) — which are represented by entities instantiating the Iconclass code that best represents them;
  • Narrative themes: these are the principal miraculous themes or dramatized events depicted on the sarcophagus panels, linked (where applicable) to the actual events to which they refer.

Together, these categories map the intersemiotic translation at the heart of the project: how a hagiographic account moves from a Literary work, becomes a Narrative theme, and is ultimately carved as a Visual item on an Artwork. Alongside these core elements, the model incorporates additional entities whose primary purpose is to provide a richer historical and semantic context to this central narrative.

Note: In the visual diagram representing this theoretical model, external linked resources (such as VIAF and Wikidata primarily used for people and works, or GeoNames for places) have been deliberately omitted for readability and to prevent visual cluttering. The only exceptions are the Iconclass codes, which are highlighted in the diagram with a purple background. For the same reason, we omitted from the diagram the detailed textual descriptions included for each entity and, where applicable, their chronological anchor.

Conceptual Model

A conceptual model was created in order to translate the theoretical abstractions into a formal, machine-readable architecture. To ensure data interoperability and alignment with Linked Open Data standards, the architecture relies on the reuse of existing vocabularies and ontologies (you can find more information about them below).

Modeling Patterns: the conceptual architecture implements standard ontology design patterns to handle the historical and artistic domain. Multiple instantiation is used to align entities across different semantic frameworks: certain nodes are declared as instances of multiple classes simultaneously (for example, combining CIDOC-CRM with LRMOO or RiC-O) to ensure interoperability. Furthermore, n-ary relations are employed to avoid semantic shortcuts when modeling complex interactions: rather than using direct binary properties, intermediate nodes (such as crm:E12_Production, lrmoo:F27_Work_Creation, or rico:CreationRelation) act as connectors between actors, objects, and their temporal constraints (e.g., crm:E52_Time-Span).

Note: Utility properties for lexical labels (RDFS, SKOS) and authority control links (OWL) are applied across all entities but are omitted from this diagram for readability.

Ontologies & Vocabularies

To accurately represent the intersemiotic translation between narrative themes, historical events, hagiographic literature, visual elements, and physical artifacts, the conceptual model adopts a multi-layered modeling strategy when needed: the narrative themes are instantiated as crm:E89_Propositional_Object and lrmoo:F1_Work, historical events as crm:E5_Event, hagiographic literature as lrmoo:F1_Work, visual elements as crm:E36_Visual_Item, and physical panels as crm:E22_Human-Made_object (which compose the sarcophagus, itself instantiated as both crm:E22_Human-Made_object and arco:HistoricOrArtisticProperty).

In order to harness this core mechanism into a more comprehensive framework, a broader network of standard ontologies and vocabularies was integrated. The specific rationale for selecting each of these models is detailed below:

Domain: Italian Cultural Heritage and contextual information

Usage rationale: ArCo (the italian standard ontology network for cultural heritage) was used to provide cataloguing information for the arca and the sarcophagus. Its application was divided into two main areas:

  • Cultural Property Classification: The main arco module was used to classify the physical artifacts according to national cataloguing standards. The monument as a whole (the Arca di San Domenico) was modeled as both an arco:HistoricOrArtisticProperty and a arco:ComplexCulturalProperty (reflecting its composite architectural nature). The sarcophagus itself is instantiated as an arco:HistoricOrArtisticProperty.
  • Contextual n-ary relations: The arco-cd module was used for modeling the patronage of the monument, linking the sarcophagus to the Dominican Order via an explicit arco-cd:Commission event node, connected through the arco-cd:hasCommission and arco-cd:hasCommittent properties.

Domain: Cultural Heritage, Physical Objects, and Historical Events.

Usage rationale: CIDOC CRM acts as the foundational ontology of the graph. Its application was divided into four conceptual macro-areas:

  • Materiality and Architecture: Used to model the physical artifacts such as the sarcophagus and its individual panels (crm:E22_Human-Made_Object). The architectural context was modeled by representing the original church (crm:E24_Physical_Human-Made_Thing) and its historical development (crm:E81_Transformation).
  • Actors and Historical Context: Used for mapping the human network and timeline. It connects historical individuals (crm:E21_Person) and collectives (crm:E74_Group) to actions (such as crm:E12_Production), events (crm:E5_Event), and their temporal relationships (crm:E52_Time-Span).
  • Semantics and Iconography: Used to map the formal visual aspects carved into the marble instantiating them as crm:E36_Visual_Item, while the abstract miracles and narrative themes they represent are modeled as crm:E89_Propositional_Object. The classification of these visual items is further formalized using crm:E55_Type to accommodate iconclass identifiers.
  • Textual sources: Applied to identify the historical, literary and archival sources (crm:E31_Document) which contain the original nuclei of the narrative themes handed down by Dominican hagiography.

Domain: Standardized Metadata, Authorship, and Multilingual Descriptions

Usage rationale: Dublin Core was used mainly as vocabulary for assigning literal metadata to the entities. Its application was focused on:

  • Multilingual Entity Descriptions: Every entity in the graph is enriched with a descriptive text using the dcterms:description property. To ensure accessibility, these strings are typed with language tags (@en and @it).
  • Bibliographic Attributes: Used to attach essential metadata to the literary works and documents, such as identifying the authors (dcterms:creator) and binding the entities to rdfs:Literal values, specifically their titles (dcterms:title) and original languages (dcterms:language).

Domain: Bibliographic Genesis and Literary Evolution

Usage rationale: LRMoo was critical for modeling the textual sources, particularly the complex literary lineage of Saint Dominic's hagiographies. Its application was divided into two fundamental aspects:

  • Literary work Creation: The literary sources are modeled exclusively at the intellectual level (lrmoo:F1_Work), maintining focus on the narrative transmission of thematic contents.
  • Literary Succession and Inspirations: To map the subsequent choices of the official hagiographies, they are linked in a direct sequence using the lrmoo:R1_is_logical_successor_of property. Furthermore, the influence of one work on another are captured using the lrmoo:R2_is_inspired_by property.

Domain: Archival Description, Documentary Hierarchies, and Institutional Roles.

Usage rationale: While LRMoo was used to model the literary texts, Ric-o handles official archival documents and institutional structures. Its application focuses on the legal and historical context of the records, divided into three main areas:

  • Archival Hierarchies: Applied to the primary documentary entity, Saint Dominic's Canonization Process (the Acta Canonizationis). Rather than an intellectual work, this is modeled as an archival unit (rico:Record). Its internal structure is mapped using rico:hasDirectConstituent to devide the main document into its distinct sections (rico:RecordPart), specifically the canonical inquiries of Bologna and Toulouse.
  • N-ary Relations for Archival Documents: Two accurately model two friar's depositions, an n-ary relation (rico:CreationRelation) was employed: it connects the document (rico:relationHasSource) to the specific friar (rico:Person via rico:relationHasTarget), explicitly defining their role as witness using rico:crationWithRole linked to a rico:RoleType.
  • Institutional Structure: Ric-o was also used to map the administrative hirerarchy within the Dominican Order. The relationship between the Master Generals and the Order itself (rico:Group) is formalized through the rico:isOrWasLeaderOf property.

Domain: Controlled Vocabularies, Authority Files, and Lexical Labels.

Usage rationale: SKOS was integrated to manage terminological alignments and lexical variations. Its application was focused on three distinct interoperability tasks:

Authority Control Alignment: Used to map entities to their corresponding records in the Virtual International Authority File (VIAF).

Vocabulary Integration: Employed to connect the iconographic classes (crm:E55_Type) to the official Iconclass classification codes, ensuring interoperability with external art history databases.

  • Lexical Variations: The skos:altLabel property was utilized to handle alternative naming conventions for major entities (such as the Arca itself or the Dominican Order), tagged with @en and @it language identifiers to support multilingual queries.

    • Domain: Art History Iconographic Classification.

    Usage rationale: ICONCLASS was utilized to provide a standardized system for categorizing and retrieving iconographic content, facilitating cross-institutional and cross-language searches within the art history domain. Its implementation includes a specific methodological adaptation:

    Standard Classification: Employed to assign persistent URIs to recognized iconographic themes, linking the local visual items (crm:E36_Visual_Item) to global art history registries.

    Custom Local Extension: While the majority of the themes were mapped to official Iconclass URIs, a custom identifier (11H_REGINALD) was deliberately minted for the iconographic representation of Reginald of Orléans. Since this specific historical figure lacked a granular official code, this local extension was created to rigorously connect the figure to the visual details in which he appears in the panel #panel_story_reginald, utilizing the crm:P138_represents property.

    Domain: Semantic Web, Knowledge Representation, and Ontology Engineering.

    Usage rationale: While OWL is a highly complex web ontology language, its implementation in this project it was used strictly for identity resolution across the Semantic Web. By utilizing the owl:sameAs property, the local entities were asserted to be absolutely identical to universal URIs from major global authority controls that model real-world entities, specifically GeoNames (for spatial coordinates) and Wikidata.

    Domain: Basic Semantic Structuring and Multilingual Labeling.

    Usage rationale: While more complex descriptions were handled via Dublin Core, RDFS was strictly utilized for its fundamental and universally readable properties. Specifically, it was employed to assign basic human-readable labels (rdfs:label) to the entities. These labels were tagged with appropriate language identifiers (@it, @en, @la).

    Data & Pipeline

    XML/TEI source file

    The textual sources were semantically enriched using the TEI (Text Encoding Initiative) guidelines. Rather than encoding the entire publications, the process focused on targeted excerpts (extracted from Luigi Canetti's L'invenzione della memoria. Il culto e l'immagine di Domenico nella storia dei primi frati predicatori and Anita F. Moskovits' Nicola Pisano's Arca di S. Domenico and its legacy) that act as the primary sources for the project's goal.

    Within the body of the XML document, the text was marked up to identify the core categories. To ensure structural traceability, each extracted passage was assigned a unique @xml:id and sequentially numbered using an @n attribute. Furthermore, the physical layout of the original printed publications was carefully preserved: page boundaries were marked using <pb>, while any internal text omissions or authorial footnotes were systematically tracked using <gap reason="omitted"> and <ptr type="footnote" n="..."/> respectively.

    Explicit mentions of historical figures, literary works, visual item, or narrative theme was wrapped in its appropriate TEI element (e.g., <persName>, <title> or <placeName>). However, when the authors employed an indirect reference rather than an explicit name, the <rs> (referencing string) element was used, indicating its entity type (e.g., <rs type="person">, <rs type="narrative_theme">,<rs type="object">, <rs type="org">, <rs type="event"> or <rs type="place">). Regardless of the specific tag used, each marked entity was equipped with a ref attribute pointing to a unique local identifier.

    In order to establish true entities, all tagged elements were formally defined in dedicated lists (such as <listBibl>,<listPerson>, <listOrg>, <listObject>, <listPlace> or <listEvent>) within the TEI Header. These definitions provide the standard labels, descriptions, the authority control (if present) and the base URIs that are eventually parsed into the RDF graph. Both the standard labels and descriptions are provided in english and italian.

    The final step was to establish the semantic links between the defined entities directly within the TEI file. The use of the TEI relation element (<relation active="..." name="..." passive="..."/>) served as the structural blueprint for the triples generated in the final LOD dataset.

    JSON Dataset

    While the TEI-XML framework defines and captures the entities explicitly mentioned in the literary sources, the conceptual model requires a broader scope to fully map the intersemiotic translation. To bridge this gap, a supplementary JSON dataset was implemented to define these extra entities and establish their connections to the textual data.

    The JSON file acts as an extended authority list. It formally defines all the entities that were considered worth defining but were not directly tagged in the XML snippets. This primarily includes the visual breakdown of the sarcophagus' panels (either in compositions or as individual details) and the narrative themes reified in marble. Each entry in the JSON object is assigned a unique identifier, two labels (in Italian and English), one or more RDF class types, two descriptions (in Italian and English), and, when possible, one or more external links (authority controls). The relations are structured as follows: the subject, the predicate (RDF property) and the object; subject and object are two dictionaries containing the identifier and whether the entity is defined in the XML or in the JSON dataset.

    XSLT: XML to HTML conversion

    To facilitate the exploration of the TEI-XML file, a conversion process was implemented to transform the XML data into an HTML format through an XSLT (eXtensible Stylesheet Language Transformations) stylesheet. This conversion allows for a more user-friendly presentation of the content, enabling users to navigate through the textual sources and their associated entities with ease. The resulting HTML file includes metadata cards for the two sources, the snippets extracted and encoded, a tab with metadata cards for all the "core" entities extracted and a "relations table" showing their relationships as triples.

    A dedicated Python script was also implemented to automate the conversion process.

    Semantic Data Extraction: RDF and Turtle Serialization

    The final step of the project involved the extraction of semantic data from the TEI-XML and JSON files, resulting in a comprehensive RDF dataset. While this project does not deploy a live graph database (Triplestore) with a SPARQL endpoint, the underlying data architecture is fully graph-based and can be easily imported into any RDF-compliant database for further exploration and querying. A dedicated Python script was implemented to automate the extraction process of the triples, maintaining interoperability with other Linked Open Data resources. These resulting triples were then serialized in Turtle format (.ttl). The dataset was finally fed into an RDF visualizer in order to make the semantic network visually accessible, as illustrated below.

    Technologies and tools

    The project was developed using a combination of web technologies, programming languages, and libraries to ensure data persistence, scalability, and semantic interoperability. The following technologies were employed:

    Data Modeling
    • TEI-XML: Employed for the structural markup of the source texts and to formally define the "core entities" and their relationships directly within the primary sources.
    • JSON: Used as a supplementary dataset to create and manage extra contextual entities and their semantic relationships outside the textual flow.
    • RDF: The underlying framework of the knowledge graph, ultimately serialized in Turtle (.ttl) format.
    Processing Pipeline
    • Python: The core scripting language orchestrating data extraction and automation, utilizing:
      • rdflib: To extract triples from both XML and JSON, and serialize the final graph.
      • lxml & xml.etree.ElementTree: To parse the XML documents and apply the stylesheet transformations.
      • BeautifulSoup4 (bs4): To format and pretty-print the generated HTML output.
      • Jinja2: To dynamically generate the interactive entities tab from the raw datasets.
    • XSLT: The stylesheet language explicitly used to define the transformation rules from the TEI-XML structure into readable HTML.
    UI & Visualizations
    • HTML5 & Bootstrap 5: Utilized to design the responsive documentation page and interactive UI components.
    • yEd & Graffoo: Standard yEd Graph Editor was used to map the theoretical model, while its Graffoo (Graphical Framework for OWL Ontologies) extension was strictly employed to draw the conceptual model, ensuring a standardized visual representation of the RDF architecture.
    • RDF Visualizer: Integrated to provide a graphical node-based rendering of the final .ttl graph.
    • Interactive Diagram Viewer: A custom JavaScript tool implemented to enable interactive zooming and panning of the SVG models (Theoretical Model, Conceptual Model, and RDF network). Originally adapted with permission from Alessandro Rocchi's repository, it was extended by integrating a custom full-screen mode.
